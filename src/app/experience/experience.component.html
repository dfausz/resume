Moultrie Mobile
Xamarin MAUI .NET C# LINQ Android iOS Mobile REST JSON

A mobile hunting and outdoor companion app (iOS & Android), built first in Xamarin and later migrated to .NET MAUI. Available on the App Store and Google Play.

Early Contributions – Weather & Navigation
My first project was the Daily Weather page, where I worked closely with UX designers to turn wireframes into a clean, user-friendly forecast view. Not long after, I became the lead on the redesign of the app’s top-level navigation, implementing a custom renderer to deliver the flow UX envisioned, which was a tough but rewarding challenge.

Collaborating with UX
I contributed heavily to the internal UI showcase app, helping define its structure and patterns so the UX team could preview components outside of the production codebase. I also chipped in on smaller fixes and functionality across the app and website.

Core Feature Work – Image Gallery
When the team reorganized into feature-based squads, I joined the Image Gallery team, which owned the app’s most important feature. I helped with the UI redesign, worked on the complex filtering logic, and contributed to supporting pieces like the feeder installation instructions.

Platform Migration – Xamarin to .NET MAUI
One of the largest initiatives during my time was the full migration from Xamarin.Forms to .NET MAUI. I was a key contributor in this effort, especially when it came to platform-specific code and ensuring stability throughout the transition.

Reflection
Moultrie Mobile was my first senior development role, and it gave me the chance to solve difficult technical problems, from framework migration challenges to handling large volumes of image data. Unlike earlier projects, I was able to see real-time feedback from a large customer base, which made the work especially rewarding. I also grew into leadership responsibilities: mentoring junior developers and interns, co-leading Clean Code lunch-and-learns, and helping set quality standards for the team. This role cemented my confidence in tackling complex problems while supporting and teaching others.

HxGN SDx
Angular HTML CSS JavaScript TypeScript

A cloud-based SaaS application built on top of Hexagon’s enterprise infrastructure solution, SmartPlant Foundation.

Building an Angular App from Scratch
Our team was tasked with creating a standalone Angular application that would integrate into the larger SDx ecosystem. Although I had only minimal Angular experience at the start, I jumped in quickly as our team took Angular classes together and began shaping the application’s foundation.

Architecture & Quality from Day One
I contributed to early architectural decisions, ensuring the app was structured for long-term maintainability. From the beginning we emphasized quality: setting up unit tests and a CI/CD pipeline that enforced over 95% coverage and passing tests. I also took responsibility for configuring the linter, which kept the codebase consistent across contributors.

Enterprise Integration
Beyond building the UI itself, we needed to ensure our app could work seamlessly with Hexagon’s broader ecosystem. That required learning the internal server infrastructure, configuring SDx to recognize our component, and wiring up the systems so SDx could navigate into our app and pass along the data we needed.

Aligning with UX & Platform Standards
Finally, I worked with UX to make sure the application fit naturally into SDx. This meant adopting the look, feel, and interaction patterns of the larger platform, so our app felt like a cohesive part of SDx rather than a bolt-on.

Reflection
This project gave me hands-on experience in enterprise-scale front-end development: building a product from scratch, enforcing high-quality standards, integrating into massive infrastructure, and making UX decisions that served the whole ecosystem.

Intergraph Smart® Construction OnSite
Xamarin .NET C# LINQ Android iOS Mobile SQLite REST JSON ASP.NET

A companion mobile app for Intergraph Smart Construction that allows users to download installation work packages, head into the field, and track progress offline — syncing updates back when connectivity is available.

Early Professional Development
OnSite was my first professional feature development role, and I joined a very small team (two app developers and one infrastructure developer). This gave me the opportunity to learn quickly and contribute across the app stack, gaining deep experience with C#, SQLite, and LINQ while working on both new features and large-scale refactors.

UI Refactors & Framework Migration
I collaborated with the team and UX designers on multiple UI refactors, first prompted by a major domain logic shift and later when the desktop application adopted a new paradigm that the companion app needed to reflect. During this time, I also contributed to the upgrade from Xamarin v1 to Xamarin v5, helping adapt platform code and ensuring stability through the transition.

Offline Sync & Field Features
A major part of the app’s value was its ability to work in the field. I helped develop an improved offline sync system, ensuring crews could download work packages, track progress remotely, and sync updates later. I also contributed to enhancements like a much better PDF viewing experience, which was critical for accessing drawings and installation documents on site.

Enterprise Integration & APIs
Our team was responsible for creating the APIs that pulled data from the shared server solution used by the desktop application. I contributed to this effort by helping design endpoints and focusing on making them as RESTful and consistent as possible, while also working on the mobile-side integration that consumed them.

Team Culture & Architecture Improvements
As a small and relatively fresh team, we invested in growing together. I was especially active in working closely with UX to advocate for user-friendly design decisions, and I helped organize and lead lunch-and-learns where we studied Clean Code and The Pragmatic Programmer. I also participated in regular developer meetings as we shifted toward a proper MVVM architecture, moving away from heavy code-behind patterns.

Testing & Stability
I contributed to our growing emphasis on testing by adding unit and integration tests alongside new features and refactors. This helped strengthen the app’s stability, which was critical in field environments where downtime was not an option.

Reflection
OnSite was where I grew from a junior developer into a contributing engineer on an enterprise product. It gave me early experience in UI development, backend integration, and architecture discussions, while also letting me champion UX collaboration and team learning culture. It set the foundation for the way I approach both code quality and user experience today.
